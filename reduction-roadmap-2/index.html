Reduction Roadmap 2.0

  • The Reduction Roadmap 2.0 identifies where we are today, where we need to go, and the speed at which we must reduce our carbon emissions to get within Earth’s safe operating space. The result is a science-based transformation tool and a call-to-action for the Danish building industry and political actors, working to align all stakeholders with a commitment to the physical limits of the Paris Agreement and the Planetary Boundaries.

    The Reduction Roadmap is developed in a cross-sectoral partnership with advisors, research institutes, architects, and NGOs within the building industry and is based on the latest scientific knowledge of anthropogenic human impact on the planet. The Reduction Roadmap concept, framework, and methodology are purposefully developed to be adaptable on an international scale.

Presentation and background information are available in Danish only.

Download background info and resources

Includes over 90% of Danish new build.

In 2023, the Reduction Roadmap was updated along with the remaining global CO₂ budget. As opposed to the first roadmap, the Reduction Roadmap 2.0 not only covers new housing construction but 90% of all Danish new build.

We have to reduce to 0.3 by 2030.

The Reduction Roadmap 2.0 illustrates that the Danish building industry has to reduce its climate impact to 0.3 kg CO₂ per square meter per year no later than 2030 to align with the Paris Agreement and get within the Safe Operating Space.

This means that in 2025, we must reduce our climate impact to a maximum of 5.8 kg CO₂ per square meter per year.

Backed by 630+ firms in the industry.

The Reduction Roadmap 2.0 reached substantial political awareness as more than 630 firms, municipalities, research institutes, and organizations called for a new political CO₂ cap in the Danish building legislation based on the 2025 target in the Reduction Roadmap 2.0.

While the target was not implemented, the mobilization has changed the low-climate-impact discourse in the building industry for good.

Between October 2023 and May 2024, here is what we asked the Danish building industry to support

There is a gap between legislation and climate science in Danish construction. It requires political action. 

Right now, politicians are negotiating the Danish building code for 2025. Industry professionals behind the Reduction Roadmap have visited the Danish Parliament's Housing Committee and are invited for dialogue with several political parties. Therefore, companies and organizations now have a unique opportunity to let the industry's voices be heard before the emission requirements are established.
 
Both the Reduction Roadmap 1.0 and the Reduction Roadmap 2.0 illustrate that new construction must emit less than 5.8 kg CO₂-eq./m²/year on average in 2025 to comply with the Paris Agreement. Today, the political emission requirement is 12. 

The construction industry is already far below 12 kg CO₂-eq./m²/year. An average building emits 9.5 kg CO₂-eq./m²/year – and there are numerous examples of buildings emitting below 5.8. 

Your company/organization, together with the rest of the construction and real estate industry, has a unique opportunity to hold politicians accountable, so our building legislation will live up to climate science. No single company can tackle the task alone. 

With your support, we are simply asking for backing that political emission requirements should reflect climate science in 2025, so the entire Danish construction industry is collectively obligated to build within the limits of the Paris Agreement. We are not asking individual companies and organizations to commit to the Reduction Roadmap. 

Fill out the form and show your organization's support for the Danish emission requirements for residential, commercial, and institutional construction in 2025 to align with climate science to comply with the Paris Agreement.
